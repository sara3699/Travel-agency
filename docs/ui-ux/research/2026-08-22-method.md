# Research method, 2026-08-22

How the master UI/UX document was produced. Recorded so a future session can tell
what is evidence, what is judgment, and what was never checked.

## The brief

The operator asked for research only. No code, no scaffolding, no dependency was
added in this session. The requested output was a master UI/UX document that
governs every future build session on this folder.

The stated failure condition, in the operator's words: having the same UI that
everyone has, and not providing a unique user experience. That framing set the
research agenda. Roughly half the effort went into naming the generic default
precisely enough to avoid it, rather than into collecting best practice in the
abstract.

## Scope decisions taken before research was synthesised

Four questions were put to the operator because different answers would have
produced materially different documents.

Audience: pan-Arab, covering three markets at once. GCC outbound (Saudi Arabia,
UAE, Kuwait, Qatar), the Levant plus Egypt, and North Africa (Tunisia, Morocco,
Algeria).

Languages: Arabic and English at true parity, both first-class.

Conversion event: an enquiry over WhatsApp or a callback request, not an online
checkout.

Brand: a portfolio and demo piece, built to be seen and shared and to win client
work rather than to hit booking volume.

One conflict came out of those answers and is still open. North Africa was
included in the audience, but French, not English, is the second language of
commerce in Tunisia, Morocco, and Algeria. Arabic and English parity does not
serve that market. The open item is recorded on the project page and has to be
settled before any routing or internationalisation work starts.

## How the research ran

Sixteen research dimensions were run in parallel, grouped into five clusters:
reach, travel UX, regional, craft, and competition. Each dimension had its own
brief. No dimension was allowed to answer from model memory alone; each was
required to run live searches and read primary sources.

Every dimension then went through an adversarial check by a separate agent whose
instructions were to refute the findings rather than confirm them. That checker
was told to default to marking a claim unsupported when it could not
independently source it, and to be hostile in particular to bare statistics,
claims about a named company's current behaviour, browser support and API status
claims, legal and regulatory claims, and anything attributed to Baymard Institute
or Nielsen Norman Group without a traceable citation. Each dimension carries a
verdict of solid, mostly solid, or shaky.

A completeness critic then read every verified dimension together and answered
three questions: what nobody was assigned that matters, where two dimensions give
contradictory advice, and which ideas across the whole body of research are most
likely to defeat the sameness problem. Its identified gaps went back out to a
final round of researchers.

## What this method does not give you

The verification pass reduces fabricated statistics; it does not eliminate them.
Anything in the master document marked as reported rather than verified means a
credible secondary source said it and no primary source was read. Treat those
numbers as directional.

Published conversion benchmarks for the travel industry are unreliable in
general, because the samples are self-selected and the definitions of a
conversion differ per vendor. They are included where useful for order of
magnitude and should not be used as targets.

No competitor site was tested by a real user, and no accessibility audit was run
against a live build, because nothing is built yet. The competitive teardown
rests on reading current sites and published analysis of them, and it records
which sites could not be fetched.

Nothing here has been validated against the operator's actual inventory,
supplier relationships, or licence status, none of which exist yet.

## Where the output lives

The master document is `docs/ui-ux/2026-08-22-master-ux-doc.md`. Supporting
research, the competitive teardown, and the annotated source list sit alongside
it under `docs/ui-ux/research/`. The index at `docs/ui-ux/README.md` explains
which file answers which question.

Files here are dated. Per this folder's rules a shipped design or copy document
is not overwritten in place, so a revision creates a new dated file and the old
one stays.
