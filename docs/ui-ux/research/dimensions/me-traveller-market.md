## Summary

The Middle East is not one travel market. Three structures survive verification. First, a hyper-affluent, hyper-mobile Gulf: Saudi residents spent SAR 110.4bn abroad in 2025, up 7% on 2024, against SAR 159.9bn inbound (+4%) — the surplus is shrinking, so outbound is the faster line (SAMA via Argaam, 6 Apr 2026). UAE residents took 18.9m departures in 2019, roughly two per resident (World Bank ST.INT.DPRT, re-queried directly). Per-capita Gulf outbound spend is roughly six times the global average, not eleven; the USD 2,262 figure is unattributed and irreproducible. Second, a Levant/North Africa market that is aspiration-and-VFR, not luxury-package: on 2019 World Bank data Kuwait (~5m people) outspent Egypt (~100m) by 4.3x on outbound travel. Third, a faith-structured category with its own regulator and platform — Nusuk Umrah, under the Ministry of Hajj and Umrah, sells four package classes and requires every provider to be licensed.

The calendar is the biggest thing a Western template gets wrong, and it is worse than "use the Hijri year". Ramadan 2027 is projected 8 Feb – 8 Mar, Eid al-Fitr 9–10 Mar, Eid al-Adha 16 May — projections, announced a day ahead by moon sighting. Saudi's 1448 school year starts 23 Aug 2026 but 30 Aug in Makkah, Madinah, Jeddah and Taif. Oman's National Day holiday is set annually by decree (25–26 Nov in 2026), so holiday tables must be data-driven. The UAE's week is four-and-a-half days federally but four days in Sharjah. Ramadan redirects rather than suppresses demand: Wego measured Makkah searches +20.67% and Madinah +25.16% in 2026, ~US$400/night near the Haram, 49% booking a month or more ahead, 76% via app. Activity shifts after iftar, but the "48% of transactions between 22:00 and 02:00" figure is mobility telemetry, not commerce.

The plumbing is distinct and moving: mada is ~95% of Saudi domestic volume with mandatory least-cost routing, KNET ~80% of Kuwaiti online, OmanNet on ~95% of Omani cards, Qatar's Himyan live since Nov 2024, and the UAE's Jaywan inaugurated 20 July 2026 with Etihad accepting it from 19 August 2026. Almosafer's own Q1 2025 data — the best-evidenced item in the set — shows 70% app / 23% web / 7% retail, Apple Pay at 36% of bookings, BNPL 14%→25% in one year, debit 58%→49%, and 20% of retail stay bookings closing on WhatsApp. Seven regional currencies carry three decimal places (KWD, BHD, OMR, JOD, TND, IQD, LYD).

## Summary as first written, before verification

The Middle East is not one travel market; it is three, and a generic template flattens all three into a Western default. (1) A hyper-affluent, hyper-mobile Gulf: ~USD 2,262 outbound spend per capita against a global average of USD 197, Saudi residents spending SAR 110.4bn abroad in 2025 and UAE residents taking roughly two international trips per head. (2) A very different Levant/North Africa: Egypt (119m people, median age 24.5) spent less on outbound travel in 2019 than Kuwait (5m people) — these are aspiration-and-VFR markets, not luxury-package markets. (3) A faith-structured product category — Umrah, Hajj, halal-filtered leisure — with its own regulator, licensing, visa logic and calendar.

The calendar is the single biggest thing a Western template gets wrong. Demand is organised by the Hijri year (Ramadan 2027 ≈ 8 Feb – 8 Mar; Eid al-Fitr ≈ 9 Mar; Eid al-Adha/Hajj ≈ mid-May 2027), by school terms that differ per country (Saudi 1448: 23 Aug 2026 – 17 Jun 2027, Eid al-Adha break 11–22 May 2027), by fixed National Days clustered in Nov–Dec, and by the June–September escape from 50°C heat. Ramadan does not suppress commerce — it moves it to 22:00–02:00 and redirects it to Makkah and Madinah.

The plumbing is equally distinct: mada is ~95% of Saudi card volume, KNET/Benefit/NAPS/OmanNet dominate their markets, Apple Pay was 36% of Almosafer's bookings, BNPL jumped to 25% of them in a single year, and five regional currencies use three decimal places. WhatsApp is not a support channel, it is a booking channel — 20% of Almosafer's retail stay bookings. Discovery is social-first and Snapchat-heavy in Saudi (72.9% of the population) in a way no Western playbook anticipates.

## Findings

### Saudi residents spent SAR 110.4 billion (~USD 29.4bn) on outbound travel in 2025, up 7% on SAR 103.4bn in 2024 — the outbound line is large and growing faster than the inbound line's 4%.

Confidence: reported · type: data

Why it matters here: Sets the size of the addressable pot for a Saudi-facing package site and confirms outbound is not a residual category. It also tells you the money is in high-ticket trips, not micro-transactions — which is what makes instalments and trust signals load-bearing.

Evidence: Argaam, 'Tourist spending in Saudi Arabia hits SAR 160B in 2025', published 6 April 2026, reporting Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) balance-of-payments data: inbound SAR 159.9bn (+4%), outbound SAR 110.4bn (+7%), travel surplus SAR 49.4bn. https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1894160

### GCC outbound tourism spending averages ~USD 2,262 per capita against a global average of ~USD 197 — roughly eleven times the world norm. Qatar leads at USD 6,836 per capita, Kuwait USD 3,086, UAE USD 2,256.

Confidence: reported · type: data

Why it matters here: This is the single most important number for positioning. A Gulf-facing package site is not competing on cheapness; it is competing on curation, trust and convenience for buyers with unusual purchasing power. It also justifies inverting the usual OTA hierarchy: lead with the experience, not the price filter.

Evidence: RESo Insights, 'GCC Tourism and Spending', 2 October 2025. Article states the GCC per-capita figure is 'eleven times the global average' and gives Qatar USD 6,836, Kuwait USD 3,086, UAE USD 2,256. ### Outbound spend does not track population. On the last complete World Bank series (2019), UAE spent USD 18.4bn, Saudi USD 16.4bn, Kuwait USD 15.8bn, Qatar USD 12.5bn, Iraq USD 10.9bn, Lebanon USD 6.5bn, Egypt USD 3.7bn, Oman USD 3.4bn, Morocco USD 3.1bn, Bahrain USD 2.6bn, Jordan USD 1.6bn, Tunisia USD 0.9bn, Algeria USD 0.67bn. Kuwait (pop ~5m) outspent Egypt (pop ~119m) by more than 4x.

Confidence: verified · type: data

Why it matters here: Kills the instinct to treat 'the Middle East and North Africa' as one audience with one funnel. Kuwait, Qatar and Bahrain are tiny by headcount and enormous by wallet — they justify per-market payment rails and per-market currency work that population size alone would never justify. Iraq at USD 10.9bn is a large, almost unserved outbound market. Egypt, Morocco and Tunisia need a fundamentally different, price-led product.

Evidence: World Bank Open Data, indicator ST.INT.XPND.CD (International tourism, expenditures, current US$), queried via api.worldbank.org on 22 August 2026. NOTE: this series has no values after 2020 for these countries, so it is a structural/relative signal, not a current figure — flagged as older than 2023. https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/ST.INT.XPND.CD

Source: https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/ST.INT.XPND.CD

### Trip frequency per head differs by an order of magnitude across the region. World Bank departures data (2019): Saudi Arabia 27.2m departures, UAE 18.9m (roughly two per resident), Oman 6.6m, Algeria 5.7m, Bahrain 3.0m, Tunisia 2.8m, Morocco 2.0m (about one trip per 18 residents), Jordan 1.5m.

Confidence: verified · type: data

Why it matters here: Determines repeat-purchase design. A UAE resident is a high-frequency buyer who benefits from saved travellers, saved passports, wishlists and a loyalty loop. A Moroccan buyer is a once-every-few-years buyer for whom the product is a considered, reassurance-heavy purchase. The same interface cannot serve both well without explicit market switching.

Evidence: World Bank Open Data, indicator ST.INT.DPRT (International tourism, number of departures), queried 22 August 2026. Latest values are 2019/2020 — flagged as older than 2023. Saudi departure counts include land crossings to Bahrain/UAE, so per-capita frequency there is overstated relative to air travel. https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/ST.INT.DPRT

Source: https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/ST.INT.DPRT

### Almosafer, Saudi Arabia's largest consumer travel brand, reports that in Q1 2025 70% of bookings came through its app, 23% through web and 7% through retail — and that 15% of retail bookings and 20% of retail stay bookings are now completed on WhatsApp. Android booking volume grew 32% year on year.

Confidence: verified · type: data

Why it matters here: Two hard directives. First, mobile web must be built to app-grade quality because the app-native habit is already set; a desktop-first layout is a rounding error. Second, WhatsApp is a booking channel, not a support channel — the site must be able to hand a fully-populated cart into WhatsApp and take a payment link back, not merely display a 'Chat with us' bubble. The Android growth also rules out iOS-only performance assumptions.

Evidence: Almosafer, 'Insights & Trends of the Saudi Traveller', April 2025 (presented at Arabian Travel Market). Proprietary booking data comparing 1 Jan–31 Mar 2024 vs 1 Jan–31 Mar 2025. Figures read directly from the report's Booking Channels page. https://almosafer-corporate-website-assets-prod.almosafer.com/Travel_trends_ATM_2025_April_Eng_1107fea106.pdf

### Almosafer's payment mix shifted sharply in one year: debit card fell from 58% to 49% of bookings, credit card held at 21%, Buy Now Pay Later rose from 14% to 25% (a 79% growth in share) and loyalty fell from 7% to 5%. Apple Pay accounted for 36% of all bookings. 10% of bookings now include travel insurance or a 'Cancellation Freedom' add-on.

Confidence: verified · type: data

Why it matters here: BNPL at a quarter of bookings on a high-ticket category is not a nice-to-have; instalment pricing has to appear at the package-card level, not only at checkout, or the price looks 3–4x higher than a competitor showing 'or 4 × SAR X'. Apple Pay at 36% means the checkout must offer a one-tap wallet path before any card form. And a 10% attach rate on cancellation cover is a real, sourced revenue line worth designing for rather than bolting on.

Evidence: Almosafer, 'Insights & Trends of the Saudi Traveller', April 2025 — Payment Methods and Safeguarding Trips pages, Q1 2024 vs Q1 2025 proprietary booking data. https://almosafer-corporate-website-assets-prod.almosafer.com/Travel_trends_ATM_2025_April_Eng_1107fea106.pdf

### Every Gulf market has a dominant domestic card scheme that international checkouts routinely omit: mada carries roughly 95% of Saudi domestic POS/e-commerce and merchants above defined revenue thresholds are legally required to accept it; KNET carries over 70% of Kuwait's online transactions; Benefit routes about 60% of Bahrain's; NAPS/Qpay over 70% in Qatar; OmanNet about 70% in Oman. Saudi electronic payments reached 85% of retail transactions in 2025, up from 79% in 2024.

Confidence: reported · type: constraint

Why it matters here: A checkout that shows only Visa/Mastercard logos silently excludes the majority of each market. This is a hard gating requirement on payment-provider selection (Tap, Checkout.com, HyperPay, Moyasar all support these rails; Stripe historically does not cover KNET/Benefit). It also affects UI: these are redirect-and-OTP flows, so the checkout must survive a full page bounce and return.

### Five currencies used across this audience have three decimal places, not two: Kuwaiti dinar (KWD), Bahraini dinar (BHD), Omani rial (OMR), Jordanian dinar (JOD), Tunisian dinar (TND) — plus Iraqi dinar (IQD) and Libyan dinar (LYD). Saudi riyal, UAE dirham, Qatari riyal, Egyptian pound, Moroccan dirham and Algerian dinar use two.

Confidence: verified · type: constraint

Why it matters here: This is a concrete, silent-failure formatting trap. A hardcoded toFixed(2) or a money column stored in 'cents' turns KWD 1.250 into KWD 1.25 and quietly loses or invents value; a KWD 1,250.000 package can render as KWD 125,000.0 if the minor-unit exponent is wrong. Prices, totals, refunds and instalment splits must all derive their decimal count from an ISO 4217 minor-unit lookup, and integer money storage must scale by 10^exponent per currency, not a fixed 100.

Evidence: ISO 4217 currency code table, minor-unit ('D') column: BHD 048 = 3, IQD 368 = 3, JOD 400 = 3, KWD 414 = 3, LYD 434 = 3, OMR 512 = 3, TND 788 = 3; SAR 682 = 2, AED 784 = 2, QAR 634 = 2, EGP 818 = 2, MAD 504 = 2, DZD 012 = 2. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_4217

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_4217

### Regional BNPL is explicitly Sharia-framed and already wired into travel. Tamara advertises 'NO LATE FEES, SHARIA COMPLIANT', payment 'in up to 24 months', operates in Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait and Bahrain, and lists Travel as a served industry. Almosafer offers Tamara at up to 12 instalments at 0% interest 'with no late fees and full Sharia compliance', and Tabby at 4 instalments from a SAR 50 minimum booking. Tamara has a public partnership with Saudia for flight payments.

Confidence: verified · type: pattern

Why it matters here: It reframes instalments from a financial-distress signal (as they read in Western UX) into a religiously-compliant, mainstream convenience. The instalment badge should be presented with the same visual weight as the price and should carry the Sharia-compliance and no-late-fee claims, because those are the reasons it converts here. It also means the package price model must be divisible into clean instalments in the correct minor units.

### The forward-looking Islamic calendar for planning is: Ramadan 2027 approximately 8 Feb – 8 Mar 2027, Eid al-Fitr 2027 approximately 9 Mar 2027, Eid al-Adha 2027 approximately 16 May 2027, Hajj 2027 approximately 14–19 May 2027 with the Day of Arafah on 15 May. ### Ramadan redirects travel demand rather than suppressing it. Wego data for Ramadan 2026 among Saudi residents: Makkah accommodation searches +20.67% and Madinah +25.16% year on year; average nightly rates near the Haram approached US$400 (SAR 1,500); 49% of travellers booked more than one month in advance; 76% of bookings came through the Wego mobile app; demand peaked in the middle and final weeks of Ramadan. The site should pivot merchandising to Umrah, Makkah/Madinah stays, family and domestic packages, and post-Eid pre-booking during Ramadan, and should start the build two to three weeks before Ramadan begins. The 49%-book-a-month-ahead figure sets the campaign lead time; the 76% app share reinforces mobile-first.

### Ramadan inverts the daily activity curve. Reported GCC figures put roughly 48% of transactions between 22:00 and 02:00 during Ramadan (against 18–22% of daily movement in that window on normal days), with e-commerce rising 30–50% versus baseline months.

Confidence: reported · type: trend

Why it matters here: Peak traffic, peak support demand and peak conversion all move to late night for a month. That has direct operational consequences: WhatsApp/agent staffing hours, deploy windows (never ship at 22:00 local during Ramadan), cron and cache-warm schedules, and the timing of push/email sends. It also argues for a genuine dark theme, since the majority of the month's sessions will happen in the dark.

Evidence: Carriyo, 'Ramadan, Eid, and the 3 AM Order' ('over 48% of daily movement in the GCC happens after iftar, compared to 18-22% on regular days'), https://carriyo.com/resources/blog/ramadan-eid-and-the-3-am-order-how-middle-eastern-ecommerce- ; Memob, 'How GCC consumers move, shop and spend during Ramadan 2026' ('E-commerce rises 30–50% during Ramadan compared to baseline months'), https://www.memob.com/how-gcc-consumers-move-shop-and-spend-during-ramadan-2026/ . ### GCC summer travel has two distinct peaks, not one: the Eid al-Adha window and then the school break. Confidence: reported · type: data

USD 1,551 / 5 nights is a concrete anchor for what a mid-market package should look like, and the destination list tells you which supply to secure first.

### Fixed National Day holidays cluster in Nov–Dec and are staggered across the Gulf: Saudi National Day 23 September; Oman 25–26 November; UAE 2–3 December; Bahrain 16–17 December; Qatar 18 December. Kuwait's National and Liberation Days fall 25–26 February. Public holidays in 2026 fell on Wednesdays and Thursdays in several markets, which changes how long a break actually is.

Weekday placement matters because a Wednesday holiday plus a Fri–Sat weekend produces a five-day break in some markets and a three-day break in others.

### The working week is not uniform. Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Egypt, Jordan and Iraq have a Friday–Saturday weekend; the UAE moved to a four-and-a-half-day week in January 2022 with Friday a half day and a Saturday–Sunday weekend. Morocco, Tunisia and Lebanon run a Monday–Friday week.

Confidence: verified · type: constraint

Why it matters here: 'Weekend getaway' is a different pair of dates in every market. A date picker that highlights Saturday and Sunday as 'the weekend' is factually wrong for most of this audience. Default date ranges, 'long weekend' suggestions, and the weekly rhythm of email/push sends all have to be market-aware — and a UAE-based operator selling into Saudi has a genuinely different working week from its customers.

Evidence: Wikipedia, 'Workweek and weekend' — Saudi Arabia changed from Thursday–Friday to Friday–Saturday in 2013; Kuwait since 2007; Bahrain since 2006; Oman since 2013; Egypt, Jordan and Iraq Friday–Saturday; Morocco, Tunisia and Lebanon Monday–Friday. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workweek_and_weekend ; Khaleej Times, 'New UAE weekend and working hours in 2022' (four-and-a-half-day week, Friday afternoon plus Saturday and Sunday), https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/government/new-uae-weekend-and-working-hours-in-2022-all-your-questions-answered

### Umrah is a structurally different product with its own state platform, its own package taxonomy and mandatory licensing. Nusuk Umrah — operated under the Saudi Ministry of Hajj and Umrah — sells four package classes: comprehensive (visa included), packages without visa (for GCC citizens and existing visa holders), customisable (choose cities, adjust night counts, modify individual services) and gift packages. Its search takes arrival city (Makkah or Madinah), start date, group size and country of residence. All service providers 'must be officially licensed and approved by the Ministry of Hajj and Umrah'.

Confidence: verified · type: constraint

Why it matters here: Country of residence is a first-class search input, not a profile field, because it determines whether a visa is needed at all. The Makkah/Madinah night split and the ability to reorder cities is the core configurator, not a nice-to-have. And selling Umrah at all requires a licence — which means either a licensing path or an explicit affiliate/referral model, decided before any schema is written.

Evidence: Nusuk Umrah official platform, https://umrah.nusuk.sa/ — describes itself as 'a one-stop-shop platform' under Ministry of Hajj and Umrah supervision, lists the four package types, the search filters (arrival city, start date, group size, country of residence) and the licensing requirement. ### Halal travel is not one checkbox — HalalBooking exposes it as a dozen separable facets with a per-feature traffic-light rating. Its filters include certified halal food; alcohol-free environments and alcohol-free room options; women-only beaches, pools and spas; hotels where modest swimwear is welcomed or required; villas with secluded pools hidden from outside view; Haram-connected prayer halls; walking-distance access to holy sites; free shuttles to prayer locations; ladies' prayer area proximity in Madinah; Haram and Kaaba view rooms; south-facing rooms overlooking Masjid an-Nabawi; family room versus connected/adjacent room options; and bidet amenities. Each feature is colour-coded: green (halal-friendly for most guests), amber (might not be acceptable), red (not acceptable). Its FAQ names the user-facing filters as 'halal food', 'no-alcohol policy', 'ladies privacy' and 'review score'.

Confidence: verified · type: pattern

Why it matters here: This is the reference taxonomy and the schema should mirror it: separate nullable facets on the property, not a single boolean. The traffic-light pattern is the important idea — it lets a property be listed honestly when only some criteria are met, instead of forcing a binary halal/not-halal claim that no supplier can guarantee. It also reveals the granularity that matters: not 'prayer facilities' but 'ladies prayer area within walking distance'.

## Design implications

- Build a Money value object before anything else, keyed on ISO 4217 minor units, and forbid raw floats anywhere in the codebase. Store integer minor units plus a currency code, derive the decimal count from a lookup (KWD/BHD/OMR/JOD/TND/IQD/LYD = 3; SAR/AED/QAR/EGP/MAD/DZD = 2), and render with Intl.NumberFormat with the currency's own minimum/maximumFractionDigits. Add a Postgres CHECK constraint so a KWD amount can never be persisted with a 2-decimal scale. Write a test that formats 1250 minor units in each supported currency and asserts KWD renders as 1.250 and SAR as 12.50.
- Model a `payment_method` matrix per market and render the checkout from it rather than from a global default: Saudi → mada (co-badged) + Apple Pay + Tabby/Tamara + credit card; Kuwait → KNET + Apple Pay + Tabby; Bahrain → Benefit/BenefitPay + Tamara; Qatar → NAPS/Qpay; Oman → OmanNet; UAE → cards + Apple Pay + Tabby/Tamara; Egypt → Meeza/Fawry/InstaPay plus a pay-later-at-agent hold. Choose the PSP on this basis (Tap, Checkout.com, HyperPay, Moyasar all cover the Gulf rails). Design the checkout state machine to survive a full-page redirect and OTP round trip, because that is how these rails work — a single-page card form with no return path will lose the majority of Gulf transactions.
- Show instalment pricing at the package-card level, not at checkout. Every price renders as the full amount plus 'or 4 × SAR X — no late fees, Sharia compliant' with the provider mark, because BNPL reached 25% of Almosafer's bookings and its selling point here is religious compliance rather than affordability. The instalment split must divide cleanly in the destination currency's minor units and must recompute when currency or party size changes.
- Make party composition the first interaction on the site, not a two-adults default buried in a search bar. Build a 'Who's travelling?' builder that takes adults, children with ages, infants, grandparents, and a rooms preference (family room / connecting rooms / adjacent rooms), and let it drive room maths automatically the way HalalBooking does. Support parties well beyond four, and never price on a double-occupancy assumption — single-traveller bookings dominate volume in Saudi.
- Ship a seasonality engine as a first-class data model, not as hardcoded banner dates: a `season_window` table with (market, type, name, expected_start, expected_end, confirmed_start, confirmed_end, source). Types must include ramadan, eid_al_fitr, eid_al_adha, hajj, school_break, national_day, summer_heat_escape. Seed it with Ramadan 2027 (≈8 Feb–8 Mar), Eid al-Fitr 2027 (≈9 Mar), Hajj/Eid al-Adha 2027 (≈14–19 May / 16 May), Saudi 1448 school terms (23 Aug 2026 start, 8–16 Jan 2027 mid-year, 11–22 May 2027 Eid al-Adha break, summer from 17 Jun 2027), and the National Days (Saudi 23 Sep, Oman 25–26 Nov, UAE 2–3 Dec, Bahrain 16–17 Dec, Qatar 18 Dec, Kuwait 25–26 Feb). Lunar windows must be editable in the admin without a deploy, because moon sighting can move them by a day or two.
- Implement dual Hijri/Gregorian date display and entry, using the `islamic-umalqura` calendar in Intl.DateTimeFormat for Saudi (not a generic arithmetic Hijri calendar — they disagree). The date picker shows both systems; the confirmation email and itinerary show both; storage stays UTC ISO-8601 Gregorian.
- Make date defaults and 'weekend' logic market-aware: Friday–Saturday for Saudi, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Egypt, Jordan and Iraq; Saturday–Sunday with a half-day Friday for the UAE; Monday–Friday working week for Morocco, Tunisia and Lebanon. A 'long weekend' suggestion engine should combine the correct weekend with that market's next public holiday.
- Model halal attributes as separable, individually-sourced facets with a three-state confidence value (verified / supplier-declared / unknown) rather than one `is_halal` boolean. At minimum: halal_food_certified, alcohol_policy (none / not_in_room / served_on_site), womens_pool, womens_beach, womens_spa, modest_swimwear_policy, private_villa_pool, prayer_room, ladies_prayer_area, qibla_marked, prayer_mat_in_room, wudu_facility, bidet_in_bathroom, distance_to_haram_metres, haram_view, family_only_section, gender_segregated_facilities, ramadan_services. Render each with the green/amber/red pattern HalalBooking uses, plus the date it was last verified and by whom.
- Treat Umrah as a separate product type with its own schema and its own route, not as a hotel-plus-flight bundle. Required fields: country_of_residence (drives whether a visa is needed at all), visa_included boolean, makkah_nights, madinah_nights, city_order, transport_between_cities, Rawdah booking, ziyarah inclusions, and the operator's Ministry of Hajj and Umrah licence number. Search must ask arrival city (Makkah or Madinah), start date, group size and country of residence — mirroring Nusuk. Decide the licensing/affiliate posture before any of this is built.
- Build the WhatsApp handoff as a real feature, not a floating bubble: a deep link that carries the full cart state (package id, dates, party composition, currency) into a pre-filled message, a WhatsApp Business API webhook that can return a payment link, and a booking record that can be created and completed by an agent in chat with the same reference the customer sees on the web. Around a fifth of assisted stay bookings at Saudi's largest travel brand already close this way.
- Design for the 22:00–02:00 peak. That means a genuine dark theme (not an inverted afterthought), support and agent coverage that covers late night during Ramadan, deploy and cache-warm windows that avoid that band, and push/email send times set per market against local iftar rather than against a UTC schedule.
- Invert the standard OTA funnel: lead with the property or the experience, because 76% of surveyed UAE travellers picked their destination purely from the accommodation. The home page's primary unit should be a rich, screenshot-worthy property/package card, with destination browsing secondary.
- Build Arabic-first, not English-with-a-toggle. Next.js App Router with `[locale]` segments, `dir` set on the html element, CSS logical properties (`margin-inline-start`, not `margin-left`) throughout, an Arabic display and body face chosen and subset from the start, Arabic-Indic vs Western digit handling made an explicit choice per locale, and a lint rule banning physical direction properties. Retrofitting RTL after a Latin-first design is the most expensive avoidable rewrite in this project.
- Set a hard performance budget calibrated to the weakest market in scope, not the strongest. Saudi mobile median download is 194 Mbps; Egypt is at 82.7% internet penetration with a median age of 24.5 and far more constrained devices. If Egypt or North Africa are in scope, budget for a sub-150KB initial JS payload and server-render the package pages.
- Put the tourism licence number in the footer of every page, on every package page, and inside the generated share/OG image — it is both a Saudi regulatory expectation (Circular 24411/1447) and the strongest available differentiator against unlicensed Instagram-agency competitors. Store supplier licences with an expiry date and auto-unpublish listings whose licence lapses.
- Create a distinct VFR (visiting friends and relatives) package line for Cairo, Amman, Damascus, Istanbul, Islamabad, Dhaka, Kozhikode and Manila, with flexible date search, long-stay pricing, excess-baggage bundling and multi-passenger booking. This is a top-of-the-list demand pattern in GCC search data that no premium-leisure template addresses.
- Offer a cancellation/insurance add-on at the point of booking and design it as a real product surface rather than an upsell checkbox — a 10% attach rate is already sourced from Almosafer's Q1 2025 data, and 'Cancellation Freedom' framing outperforms 'travel insurance' framing.
- Design every package page around a deliberately-composed share region: a fixed-aspect card with the price in the viewer's currency, the halal facet badges, the dates in both calendars, the licence mark and the operator handle — rendered both as an OG image for links and as a 9:16 export for Snapchat and TikTok stories. In a market where 94% of travellers discover destinations on social and 89% are introduced to options by creators, the screenshot is the distribution unit.
- Add a per-market channel and asset plan derived from real reach: Saudi → Snapchat and YouTube first (72.9% and 79.2% of population), vertical video; UAE → Instagram and LinkedIn (70.5% and 87.6%); Kuwait/Qatar → Instagram plus Snapchat; Egypt and Morocco → Facebook and YouTube. Do not build a single cross-region social strategy, and do not centre X/Twitter, whose reach is falling in both Saudi (−6.8%) and the UAE (−6.0%).

## Anti-patterns to refuse

- Formatting all money with two decimals — a `toFixed(2)`, a `numeric(10,2)` column, or a 'store cents as integers, divide by 100' helper. This silently corrupts KWD, BHD, OMR, JOD and TND amounts, which have three decimal places under ISO 4217. It is the most common and most invisible localisation bug in this region: nothing throws, the number just becomes wrong by a factor of ten, and it appears in refunds and instalment splits before anyone notices it on a price tag.
- Merchandising against the Western retail calendar — 'Summer Sale', 'Black Friday', 'Christmas escapes', 'New Year in the sun' — while the actual demand peaks are Ramadan (Umrah and Makkah/Madinah), Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, the per-country school break and the Nov–Dec National Day cluster. Worse, a Western template treats summer as the beach season; in the Gulf June–September is the escape-from-50°C season, which is a fundamentally different product (cool climates, mountains, long-haul) sold with a different emotional pitch.
- A checkout that shows Visa, Mastercard, PayPal and nothing else. mada is ~95% of Saudi card volume with mandatory merchant acceptance above revenue thresholds, KNET over 70% of Kuwaiti online transactions, Benefit ~60% in Bahrain, NAPS in Qatar, OmanNet in Oman — and Apple Pay was 36% of Almosafer's bookings. A card-only checkout does not underperform, it excludes the majority. The related mistake is picking a PSP (typically Stripe) that cannot process KNET or Benefit at all, discovered after the schema is built.
- Treating instalments as a subprime signal to be hidden at checkout, or omitting them entirely. BNPL went from 14% to 25% of Almosafer's bookings in one year (+79% share growth) and its regional selling point is Sharia compliance and no late fees, not deferred hardship. Hiding it below the fold on a high-ticket package costs a quarter of the conversions.
- A contact form, a support email and a promise of a reply 'within 24–48 hours' as the human channel. The regional default is WhatsApp with a minutes-scale expectation, and it is a closing channel — a fifth of Almosafer's assisted stay bookings complete inside it. A floating 'Chat with us' widget that opens a generic web chat, with no cart state carried over and no way for an agent to complete the booking, is the same mistake in a nicer costume.
- A guest selector that defaults to '2 adults, 1 room' and caps at four people with no children's ages, no grandparents, no connecting-room logic. Multi-generational travel is mainstream here (31% of surveyed UAE residents travelled with both parents and grandparents within two years), and single-traveller bookings simultaneously dominate volume — so both the very small and the very large party are common and the two-adults default is the least likely case.
- Collapsing halal provision into one 'Halal-friendly ✓' badge. The real decision set is a dozen separable facets — women-only pool versus women-only beach versus private villa pool, alcohol-free property versus alcohol-free room, halal kitchen versus halal options, distance to the Haram, ladies' prayer area — and a single badge forces a binary claim no supplier can honestly guarantee. It reads as marketing rather than information, which destroys exactly the trust it was meant to build.
- English-first with Arabic as a language toggle added in a later sprint, and physical CSS directions (`margin-left`, `text-align: right`) throughout. Beyond the direct conversion cost — CSA Research found 40% will never buy from a site in another language — retrofitting RTL into a Latin-first design system is one of the most expensive rewrites available, touching every component, icon, animation direction and form layout.
- Building the social strategy around Instagram alone, or centring X/Twitter. In Saudi, Snapchat reaches 72.9% of the population against Instagram's 52.4%, and X is falling (−6.8% year on year in Saudi, −6.0% in the UAE). Meanwhile the UAE inverts the picture (Instagram 70.5%, LinkedIn 87.6%, Snapchat 44.9%) and Egypt and Morocco are Facebook-led. One region-wide social plan is guaranteed to be wrong for most of the region.
- Selling Umrah or Hajj as an ordinary flight-plus-hotel bundle. Both are regulated products: Umrah providers must be licensed by the Saudi Ministry of Hajj and Umrah, Hajj is quota-controlled and date-fixed to 8–13 Dhu al-Hijjah, and the visa requirement depends on the traveller's country of residence — which makes residence a search input, not a profile field. A generic bundle here is a legal exposure, not just a UX miss.
- Stock photography of Western couples on beaches, wine glasses at dinner, and pork in the food shots — plus the subtler version, an all-Latin type system with Arabic pasted in as a fallback font. Both signal 'this site was built for someone else and translated at you', which is precisely the generic-template failure mode to avoid.
- Hardcoding lunar holiday dates into templates or campaign configs. Ramadan and both Eids move with moon sighting and can shift by a day or two from the predicted date; a hardcoded date means a redeploy under time pressure at the single highest-traffic moment of the year.

## Differentiation moves

- Per-facet halal confidence with evidence, not a badge. Adopt HalalBooking's green/amber/red pattern but go one step further: each facet carries a dated verification, the source (site visit, supplier declaration, guest photo) and who verified it. 'Women-only pool — verified 12 Mar 2026 by our Antalya inspector' is a claim no global OTA can make and a screenshot people will actually share.
- A homepage that reorganises itself by the Hijri year. Instead of a static hero, the site's primary merchandising module is driven by the season engine: Umrah and Makkah/Madinah during Ramadan, Eid escapes as Shawwal approaches, heat-escape packages from June, National Day short breaks in November. The site visibly knows what month it is in the calendar the user actually lives in.
- Prayer-time-aware itineraries. Every package day plan shows the salah windows in destination local time, the qibla bearing from the hotel, and the walking distance to the nearest masjid or the hotel's prayer room. Nobody in mainstream travel does this well, it is cheap to compute, and it converts a package page into a genuinely useful artefact that gets saved and forwarded.
- 'Who's coming?' as the opening interaction. Replace the destination-and-dates search box with a party builder — grandparents, children with ages, mahram, connecting rooms — and let the results be genuinely filtered by whether the property can actually accommodate that group. It is a completely different first impression from every metasearch clone, and it is honest about what this audience is actually buying.
- A WhatsApp handoff that carries state both ways. The site builds the cart; the customer taps through to WhatsApp with everything pre-filled; the agent can amend and send a payment link; the booking reference is identical on both sides. Position this as the product's spine rather than a support afterthought — it is where a fifth of assisted stay bookings already happen and where the human trust this market wants actually lives.
- A 'heat escape index' for June–September: rank destinations by the temperature differential against the user's home city, alongside flight time and visa friction. It is a purpose-built answer to the region's largest seasonal motivation, it produces a genuinely novel ranking, and it is inherently screenshottable.
- Publish the seasonality data as an open, linkable public resource — a per-country travel calendar showing Ramadan, both Eids, Hajj, school terms and National Days for the next three Hijri years, with the moon-sighting caveats made explicit. This is the highest-value organic-reach asset available in this dimension: it earns links from other travel sites, HR blogs and expat forums, and it demonstrates domain competence before anyone has booked anything.
- Screenshot-first package pages. Design an explicit share region with a fixed aspect ratio, rendered as both an OG image and a 9:16 story export: price in the viewer's currency with correct minor units, the instalment split, the halal facet badges, dual-calendar dates, the licence mark and the operator handle. In a market where 94% discover destinations socially, treat the screenshot as the primary distribution format and design it deliberately rather than letting the browser crop something arbitrary.
- A serious, respectful VFR product line. Cairo, Amman, Damascus, Islamabad, Dhaka, Kozhikode and Manila are at the top of real GCC search data and are served today by nothing but bare fare comparison. A well-designed 'going home' package — flexible dates, long stays, baggage included, family group pricing, gift-delivery options — is a large, defensible position that no premium-leisure competitor will bother to take.
- Radical price legibility as a trust move: show the total in the user's own currency with the correct decimals, the instalment breakdown, what is and is not included, and the cancellation terms — all above the fold, all in Arabic first. In a category where the region's frustrations are visa processing (42%) and price fluctuation (38%), being the site that never surprises anyone at checkout is a positioning, not just a UX preference.
- Put the licence number and the Sharia-compliance statement of the payment plans on the share card itself, not just in the footer. It makes the credential travel with the screenshot, which is where the trust decision actually gets made when a package is forwarded into a family WhatsApp group.
- Build a 'gift this trip' flow, mirroring Nusuk's gift Umrah packages. Gifting is an established regional pattern that Western package sites do not model at all, it maps naturally onto Eid, and it is inherently viral — every gift is a second person introduced to the brand at a moment of goodwill.

## Open questions

- Which of the thirteen markets are actually in scope for v1? Payment rails, currency minor units, school calendars and language variants all scale roughly linearly with market count. Saudi + UAE alone is a very different build from Saudi + UAE + Kuwait + Bahrain + Egypt.
- Is Umrah in scope? It requires a Ministry of Hajj and Umrah licence for the operator and a distinct product schema. If the answer is 'later', the product-type abstraction still needs to exist now; if the answer is 'never', a lot of the seasonality work simplifies.
- Is the business model direct B2C, or agent-assisted with WhatsApp as the closing channel? The Almosafer data suggests a hybrid is the regional norm, but the two produce very different information architectures — one optimises for self-serve checkout, the other optimises for cart handoff and agent tooling.
- Which Arabic register does the copy use? Modern Standard Arabic reads as formal and pan-regional; Gulf dialect reads as native to Saudi and the UAE but foreign in Tunisia; Tunisian or Moroccan Darija reads as native there and opaque in the Gulf. This is a brand decision with real reach consequences and it should be made before any copy is written.
- No sourced figure found for the average travel party size in the GCC, despite it being the single most useful number for designing the guest selector. Worth commissioning from the operator's own booking data once live rather than guessing.
- No sourced figure found for the share of GCC package bookings that still complete through offline travel agencies versus online. Almosafer's 7% retail share is one brand's mix and almost certainly understates the market-wide offline share.
- No sourced figure found for the conversion lift attributable to BNPL specifically on travel bookings in the region — the 25%-of-bookings share proves adoption but not incrementality.
- The '78.8% of Saudi travellers go solo' Wego figure needs interpretation before it drives any design: is it counting single-passenger bookings (inflated by the expatriate VFR population) or self-described solo leisure travellers? The distinction changes whether the product should optimise for solo leisure or for single-passenger VFR.
- How many of the target properties can actually supply the halal facet data at the granularity the schema needs, and who bears the verification cost? A twenty-facet model that is 90% 'unknown' is worse than a five-facet model that is 90% verified.
- Do the GCC Grand Tours visa pilot dates hold? A Q4 2026 pilot and early-2027 rollout would make a multi-country Gulf itinerary product viable within this build's lifetime; a further slip would make it dead weight.

### Flagged as not covered

- RTL and bidirectional layout is entirely absent — the single largest UI constraint for this audience. No mention of mirrored layouts, logical CSS properties, bidi-isolating mixed Arabic/Latin strings (flight numbers, IATA codes, PNRs inside Arabic sentences), or the fact that Arabic-Indic numerals (٠١٢٣) versus Western digits is a per-market convention: Gulf and Egypt commonly use Western digits in commercial contexts, the Maghreb uses Western digits almost exclusively.
- Dual-calendar date input. The dimension names the Hijri calendar as a demand driver but never says the booking form needs a Hijri/Gregorian toggle, that Saudi government documents (Iqama, visa) carry Umm al-Qura dates the user must transcribe, or that Umm al-Qura is tabular and can differ by a day from sighted dates — which breaks naive Hijri↔Gregorian conversion around month boundaries.
- ZATCA e-invoicing (Fatoora). A Saudi travel business must issue Phase 2 compliant e-invoices with QR codes, cleared/reported through ZATCA. This is a hard build requirement for any confirmation email or receipt screen and is completely absent.
- VAT and pricing display differ sharply: Saudi 15%, Bahrain 10%, UAE 5%, Oman 5%, Egypt 14%, Qatar and Kuwait 0%. Tax-inclusive display expectations and the Saudi requirement to show VAT-inclusive prices are not covered, despite the doc covering currency decimals.
- Saudi PDPL (in force Sept 2023, enforcement from Sept 2024) and UAE PDPL — consent, data-subject rights, and cross-border transfer rules that constrain where traveller PII can be stored and how consent UI must work. No privacy or data-residency constraint appears anywhere in the dimension.
- National identity SSO: Nafath (Saudi) and UAE Pass are the expected login/verification rails for anything touching government or licensed services, including Nusuk. Absent.
- Name and passport data modelling. Arabic naming (given + father + grandfather + family), transliteration variance, and MRZ-versus-Arabic-form mismatch are a top cause of failed airline ticketing and check-in for this audience. Not mentioned.
- Hajj is treated as interchangeable with Umrah. Hajj has a separate quota system, separate Nusuk Masar/Hajj portal, per-country allocations and a lottery in some markets — you cannot sell it like a package. Also unmentioned: since 2022 many nationalities can perform Umrah on an ordinary Saudi tourist e-visa, which changes the visa step in the funnel entirely.
- Address and phone conventions: no postal codes in routine use in several markets, Saudi's National Address (short address code) format, and the fact that a phone number is often the primary account identifier rather than email.
- GMTI 2026 supersedes the cited 2025 edition, including two directly design-relevant findings the doc misses: 80% of Muslim travellers now use AI tools to plan, and women drive close to half of arrivals.
- Prayer times and qibla as product features, and the Hijri-aware handling of check-in/check-out around Fajr — HalalBooking's filter list is covered but the time-of-day product surface is not.
- Almosafer's own carrier data (PDF page 7) shows a 60/40 FSC/LCC split domestically inverting by destination band, and 24% of round-trips are mix-and-match across airlines — a concrete search/results-page requirement the researcher extracted nothing from.
- Nothing on accessibility or on low-bandwidth/older-device performance for Egypt and Morocco, where internet penetration (82.7% Egypt) and device mix differ sharply from the Gulf's 99%.
